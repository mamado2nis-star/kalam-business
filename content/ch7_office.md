# Chapter 7 — Office Talk

## 1. Office etiquette, part 1: everyday office situations (p. 299–303)
Audio: ot_299

This chapter explores the nuances of office etiquette, the unwritten rules that govern behavior in the workplace.
It addresses various situations, such as punctuality, interpersonal communication, language barriers, phone etiquette and handling sensitive topics.
The aim is to equip readers with practical phrases and appropriate responses to maintain professionalism and respect in the workplace.
### Situation: Arriving late to work
I apologize for being late. I was held up in a previous meeting.
I apologize for running late. I was caught in traffic.
### Situation: Dealing with a colleague who often arrives late
Could you please try to be on time for meetings? It's important for our team.
Being late affects our work. Let's aim to start on time.
### Situation: Taking a phone call in a shared workspace
Excuse me, I need to take this call. I'll step outside to avoid disturbing anyone.
I have an important call coming in. I'll be back in a few minutes.
### Situation: Dealing with loud personal calls in the office
I can see you're on a personal call, but could you please keep it down a little? Thank you.
Would you mind taking your call outside? It's a bit disruptive. Thanks.
### Situation: Asking a colleague to lower their noise level
Could you please lower your voice a bit? I'm having trouble focusing on my work.
Would you mind turning the volume down? It's a bit distracting.
### Situation: Requesting assistance from a coworker
Do you have a moment to help me with this task? I'm not sure how to do this. Could you show me?
### Situation: Interrupting a colleague who is busy
Sorry to interrupt, but I have a quick question, if you have a moment.
### Situation: Thanking a colleague for their help
Thank you for your assistance. I really appreciate your support.
### Situation: Apologizing for a mistake
I'm really sorry, that was my fault. I'll correct the issue immediately.
I'm sorry for the error. I'll make sure it doesn't happen again.
### Situation: Apologizing for not completing tasks on time
I sincerely apologize for not completing my tasks on time. I understand the impact this may have caused. Rest assured, I am committed to finishing them promptly.
### Situation: Respecting others' workspace
Could you please keep your belongings on your side of the desk? We need to keep our shared space tidy.
### Situation: Encountering poor hygiene in the office
Our workspace is shared. Let's try to keep it fresh and clean for everyone's benefit.
### Situation: Declining an invitation to lunch or coffee
I appreciate the offer, but I have an important commitment. Maybe next time?
### Situation: Sharing office equipment or resources
Do you mind if I borrow this? I'll return it as soon as I'm done.
### Situation: Offering help to a coworker
If you need any assistance, please don't hesitate to ask. I'm here to help.
### Situation: Apologizing for a misunderstanding
I'm sorry for the confusion. I must have misunderstood your instructions.
### Situation: Clarifying instructions or expectations
Could you please walk me through this process one more time? I want to make sure I'm on the right track.
Can you confirm the next steps for me?

## 4. Office etiquette, part 4: Dialogue 1 (p. 311–313)
Audio: ot_311

The following dialogue is between three colleagues, Emily, Mark and Laura. They all work in the same team in a software development company.
Emily: Good morning, everyone! Hope you all have a productive day.
Mark: Good morning, Emily.
Laura: Good morning, Emily. You seem to be in a good mood.
Emily: Indeed. A fresh start, right?
Emily: Excuse me, I need to take this call. I'll step outside to avoid disturbing anyone.
Mark: Laura, do you have a moment to help me with this task?
Laura: Sure, Mark. What do you need help with?
(Emily comes back after the call.)
Mark: I'm sorry to interrupt, but I have a quick question, if you have a moment.
Emily: Of course, Mark. What's up?
Mark: I'm not sure how to use this new project management software. Could you please walk me through this process one more time?
Emily: Of course, Mark. Let's go through it.
(In the middle of the day)
Laura: I'm stepping out for a quick break. I'll be back shortly.
Mark: Enjoy your break, Laura.
(A few moments later, Emily's phone rings again.)
Emily: It's a personal call.
Mark: I can see this is a personal call.
Emily: I'll take this outside to avoid any distractions.
Mark: Thanks, Emily.
(Later, Emily helps Mark with the presentation.)
Mark: Thank you for your assistance, Emily. I really appreciate your support.
Emily: Any time, Mark. If you need any further assistance, please don't hesitate to ask. I'm here to help.
Laura: It's good to see such a cooperative team.
Laura: By the way, I would like to request some time off next month for a vacation. Could we discuss this when you have a moment, Emily?
Emily: Absolutely, Laura. Let's figure out the best time.
(As the day ends)
Mark: I'm heading out now.
Emily: Good night, Mark.
Mark: See you tomorrow, Emily.
Laura: See you.
This dialogue exhibits how office etiquette contributes to a respectful and professional workplace, with each character using polite and considerate expressions.
